How this works
  • Protein per £ = protein per 100 g × 10 ÷ price per kg. Clubcard mode uses the Clubcard price where there is one. A multibuy ("Any 3 for £5") counts as the price per item when you buy the full deal. Meal deals are ignored.
  • Protein share of calories = 4 kcal × grams of protein ÷ kcal. It filters out cheap but impractical protein. Rough values: potatoes 11%, flour and bread 12–13%, peanuts 18%, milk 29%, lentils 30%, eggs 38%, chicken breast 90%.
  • Best value products are on the frontier: no other matching product has both more protein per £ and a higher protein share. The frontier is recomputed for whatever you filter.
  • Values come from the nutrition label: the per 100 g (or per 100 ml) column, with 1 litre counted as 1 kg. If the label gives cooked values and says how much the food weighs once cooked ("400g typically weighs 354g"), the values are converted back to as-sold weight. Otherwise the product gets a cooked values badge.
  • Items sold "each" (eggs, rolls, sandwiches) have no pack weight from Tesco. Where possible, the weight is estimated from the label's serving size and the count in the title, and the product gets a ~weight badge.
  • Suspect data: the macros add up to more than 100 g, the listed kcal don't match protein, carbs and fat, or the price per kg is implausible. These products are usually label errors on Tesco's site.
  • Labels give nutrition for the edible part, but bone-in meat and eggs in a weighed pack are sold by weight including the bone or shell. Price per kg and protein per £ leave out a typical share for it (wings 46%, drumsticks 30%, whole birds 32%, eggshell 12%), and the product gets a minus bone or minus shell badge.

Not affiliated with Tesco. Prices and labels change, so check the product page before you rely on a number.
